## Voice and Tone

Microsoft's three voice principles:

1. **Warm and relaxed** - Natural, everyday conversation.
2. **Crisp and clear** - To the point, scanning first, reading second.
3. **Ready to lend a hand** - Anticipate needs, offer information at the right time.

Style tips:

- Get to the point fast. Lead with what matters most.
- Use short, everyday words.
- Simpler is better. Prune every excess word.
- Use active voice.

**NOTE:** The MWSG encourages contractions for friendliness. The WWL Exam Guide **prohibits** contractions in exams. WWL takes precedence. See `conflicts-and-precedence.md`.

## Capitalization

### Sentence-Style (Default)

- Capitalize the first word of a sentence, heading, title, or UI label.
- Capitalize proper nouns (brands, products, services).
- Lowercase everything else.

### Rules

- Do not use ALL CAPS for emphasis (italic is acceptable sparingly).
- Do not use all lowercase as a design choice.
- Do not use internal capitalization (AutoScale, e-Book) unless it is a brand name.
- Do not capitalize the spelled-out form of an acronym unless it is a proper noun.
- When words are joined by a slash, capitalize after the slash if the word before it is capitalized: Country/Region.

### Title-Style (Rare in Exams)

Only for: product/service names, book/song titles, article titles in citations, people's titles.

- Capitalize first and last words.
- Do not capitalize a, an, the (unless first/last).
- Do not capitalize prepositions of four or fewer letters (on, to, in, up, of, for) unless first/last.
- Do not capitalize and, but, or, nor, yet, so unless first/last.
- Capitalize all other words including verbs (is, be), adverbs, adjectives, pronouns.

## Punctuation

### Serial/Oxford Comma

Always include a comma before the conjunction in a list of three or more: "Android, iOS, and Windows."

### Periods

- End all sentences with a period, even short ones.
- One space after periods, question marks, colons.

### Lists

- Include a colon at the end of a phrase that introduces a list.
- If list items complete the introductory phrase, end each with a period.
- If all items are short phrases (three words or fewer), no periods needed.
- If any item is a complete sentence, end every item with a period.

### Colons

Lowercase the word after a colon unless it is a proper noun or quoted material.

### Semicolons

Avoid. Rewrite as two sentences or a list.

### Hyphens

Use hyphens only where omitting them causes confusion. Hyphenate:

- Compound modifiers before a noun: "well-known author."
- Spelled-out fractions: "one-third."
- Compound numbers: "twenty-one."

### Em Dashes

No spaces around em dashes.

### Slashes

Do not use slash as substitute for "or." Do not use as general punctuation.

### Quotation Marks

- Closing quotes outside commas and periods.
- Closing quotes inside other punctuation.
- Exception: if punctuation is part of the quoted material, place it inside.

### Exclamation Points

Use sparingly.

## Grammar and Parts of Speech

### Verbs

- Present tense preferred.
- Active voice preferred. Passive voice acceptable to avoid blaming user or when actor is unknown.
- Imperative mood for instructions.
- Match verb with subject in person and number.

### Pronouns

- Second person ("you") most of the time.
- Do not use gender-specific singular pronouns (he/she) in generic references.
- Do not use "they" for a single subject; recast: "A user who logs on will..." or "When users log on, they will..."

### Modifiers

- Place modifiers close to the words they modify.
- Avoid dangling participles.
- Avoid stacks of more than two modifiers per noun.

### Prepositions

- Avoid chains of consecutive prepositional phrases.

### Articles

- Do not drop articles. Include "a," "an," "the" as needed.
- Use "a" or "an" based on pronunciation: "an ISP," "a SQL database."

## Numbers

### Spelled Out

- Zero through nine (unless space-limited).
- At the beginning of a sentence.
- Ordinal numbers (first, second). No "-ly" suffix (not "firstly").

### Numerals

- 10 or greater.
- Measurements, dimensions, time, percentages.
- Numbers the customer types.
- When any number in a category requires a numeral, use numerals for all.

### Commas in Numbers

- Use for numbers with four or more digits.
- Exception: years and baud with fewer than five digits.

### Dates

- Numeral day, spelled-out month: "June 1" not "June first."

### Ranges

- Use "from" and "through" to describe ranges.
- Use en dash only in page ranges or space-limited contexts.

### Percentages

- Use percent sign with numerals: "50%" not "50 percent."

## Word Choice

### Prefer Simple Words

| Use | Not |
|-----|-----|
| use | utilize, make use of |
| remove | extract, take away, eliminate |
| tell | inform, let know |
| to | in order to, as a means to |
| also | in addition |
| connect | establish connectivity |
| because | since (when meaning "because") |

### Avoid Unnecessary Adverbs

Omit: quite, very, quickly, easily, effectively -- unless essential to meaning.

### Consistency

Use one term for one concept throughout. Do not alternate between synonyms.

### No Verbs as Nouns or Vice Versa

| Use | Not |
|-----|-----|
| affect performance | impact performance |
| download the paper | get the download |
| respond to the request | respond to the ask |

### US Spelling

Use US spelling: "license" not "licence."

### Avoid Non-English

Avoid Latin abbreviations:

- "for example" not "e.g."
- "that is" not "i.e."
- "therefore" not "ergo"

Exception: "etc." is acceptable when space is limited.

## Formatting Text

### UI Elements

- **Bold** for UI elements users interact with: button names, menu items, dialog box titles, checkbox names.
- Do not bold window or page names unless users interact with them.

### Text in Instructions

- Bold the name of the UI element: "Select **Save**."
- Do not bold punctuation after UI element names.
- Use "greater than" angle brackets for menu sequences: "Select **Accounts** > **Other accounts** > **Add an account**." (Brackets not bold, spaces around brackets.)

### Code and Developer Text

- Code style (monospace) for: code, commands, attributes, values, parameters, operators, XML/HTML elements.
- Regular text for: APIs referred to conceptually.

### Key Names

Per WWL Guide: ALL CAPS for key names (TAB, CTRL+ALT+DELETE). Note: MWSG uses different formatting but WWL takes precedence for exams.

## Accessibility and Bias-Free Communication

### People-First Language

- Default to people-first: "person with a disability" not "disabled person."
- Use identity-first only when you know the audience prefers it.

### Avoid

- "special needs"
- Gender-specific pronouns in generic references
- Culturally specific references without explanation
- Ableist language ("lame," "blind spot" in non-literal contexts)

### Neurodivergent Considerations (from WWL)

- Use consistent writing style and visual presentation across questions of the same type.
- Clear visual delineation between scenario, question, and instructions.

## UI Interaction Verbs

Use input-neutral verbs:

| Verb | Use For |
|------|---------|
| Open | Apps, files, folders, shortcut menus |
| Close | Apps, dialog boxes, windows, files |
| Leave | Websites, webpages |
| Go to | Menus, tabs, ribbons, specific UI locations |
| Select | Options, values, links, menu items |
| Select and hold | Press-and-hold actions |
| Clear | Removing checkbox selection |
| Choose | Exclusive options (only one value) |
| Enter | Typing or inputting a value |
| Move | Dragging, pasting from one place to another |

Avoid: press, press and hold, right-click, click (when possible -- use select instead).

## Technical Terms

- Use common words when possible.
- Define technical terms in context if the audience might not understand.
- Use terms consistently across the exam.
- Do not create new terms when existing ones suffice.
- Use industry-standard terminology for professional audiences.
- Check the A-Z word list and Merriam-Webster for preferred usage.
